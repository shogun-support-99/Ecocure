01 Flora

The quiet
architects.

Plants make the air, the shade and the soil. A single mature oak can host more than two thousand species — an entire ecosystem wearing bark. We survey them crown to root, then let the woodland close around them.

80%of terrestrial biomass is plant life
2,300species can live on one old oak

02 Fauna

The ones who
carry the seed.

Animals are the forest's couriers. A fox carries burrs in its coat; a deer prunes the understory; a blackbird drops a berry a kilometre away. Every movement is a small act of planting — which is why we map the paths, not just the species.

38wildlife corridors mapped and protected
214species on the station's nightly survey

03 Understory

The internet is
older than us.

Beneath the leaf litter, fungal threads trade water, carbon and warning signals between trees. Ninety percent of land plants live in partnership with fungi. The forest has been networked for four hundred million years — we are late to the conversation.

400Myears of mycorrhizal partnership
90%of land plants depend on fungal partners

04 Care

Rewilding,
measured.

We buy the marginal field, remove the drain, plant the thorn and wait. Then we count — beetles, bats, mycorrhizae, night sound. Care is not a gesture. It is a practice of attention, repeated until the place can look after itself.

12,400hectares in restoration
214partner farms & guardians
96percent sapling survival
